  #### Preface: The Blueprint of a Mind

*“When the bottom falls out of your world, most people reach for a hug or a sympathetic ear. I pulled out a mathematical equation. It was the absolute definition of trying to map the unmappable—applying engineering logic to the heart.”*This volume serves as a structural blueprint and post-mortem analysis of a life processed entirely through the mechanics of systems engineering, thermodynamic limits, and mathematical risk management. When human reality collapses into unbuffered turmoil—whether through domestic violence, systemic deceit, sudden physical trauma, or relational sabotage—the analytical mind instinctively seeks refuge in universal, non-negotiable physical laws. Mathematics does not lie; physical mechanics harbor no hidden motives; and structural equations yield deterministic outputs based on verified inputs.Throughout my life, the overarching conflict has been an uncompromising tension between raw emotional chaos and absolute logical order. When baseline operating systems are written in childhood environments defined by extreme unpredictability, the prefrontal cortex adapts by constructing cognitive firewalls, treating psychological trauma as system bugs requiring explicit algorithmic debugging. From calculating the exact pharmacokinetic bioavailability of prescription opioids to constructing game-theoretic utility models to patch corrupted lines of romantic trust, this autobiography documents a lifelong attempt to engineer equilibrium from entropy.To understand how a mechanical engineer comes to process the soul as a complex control system, one must first examine the volatile structural foundation upon which his earliest memories were constructed.

#### 1\. Foundational Architecture & Childhood Volatility

##### **~~1.1 Roots and Domestic Friction: The Cairo-New York Synthesis~~**

The structural integrity of my early childhood was compromised at its inception by an immediate, high-pressure family dynamic. My father, Karam Mina, was born in Cairo, Egypt, embodying a rigid, traditional framework. My mother, Kathie Eberhardt, was born in New York State. The two met roughly a year before my birth and were swiftly married upon learning that Kathie was pregnant with me. This immediate union between two culturally distinct individuals created a high-stress domestic climate from day one.Major macro-level economic and physical shocks rapidly accelerated the household's instability. Karam was employed at Eastman Kodak in Rochester, New York—a company that represented the regional economic baseline. However, he was laid off, an event he accepted gladly due to his conviction that the organization was deeply corrupt. Shortly after this economic disruption in the early 1990s, a severe physical trauma occurred: my father suffered an accident on a swing set that resulted in breaking both of his ankles simultaneously.Immobilized at home, Karam taught Kathie computer and office skills. Armed with this training, Kathie secured employment with the town and later transitioned into a county worker role. This inverted traditional household roles overnight: my father remained at home managing homeschooling duties for myself, my younger brother Jon (six years younger), and my sister Becky (ten years younger), while my mother became the primary outward earner.Compounding this environmental volatility was an irreplaceable personal loss. When I was three years old, my parents had a baby girl. Medical professionals initially warned she would be stillborn; though she survived birth, she suffered from a critical heart valve defect where a valve was positioned on the wrong side. She passed away two years later, when I was five years old and she was just two.

##### **~~1.2 Physical and Emotional Bunkers: Structural Adaptations to Conflict~~**

As domestic friction escalated, the family home ceased to function as a safe environment and transformed into an active conflict zone. Arguments between my parents were severe, unyielding, and constant. Karam’s primary domestic coping mechanism was absolute disengagement: walking away from confrontations and giving my mother the silent treatment, sometimes for days on end. This created an atmosphere of heavy, unspoken tension that frequently exploded into screaming matches.To adapt to this environmental instability, my father and I physically refurbished the basement, constructing a subterranean bunker complete with living spaces to physically separate ourselves from the verbal destruction upstairs. When environmental stress exceeded the containment threshold of the basement bunker, tactical evacuations were executed. My mother would scream,  *"You're not taking Nate with you\!"*  as my father and I retreated to his car, locking the doors. In an era before mobile text messaging and cellular tracking, entering a vehicle and driving to a local Burger King meant dropping entirely off the grid until the domestic system cooled down.Police interventions became a recurring metric of household dysfunction. Law enforcement officers repeatedly entered the residence, explicitly warning my parents:  *"If we have to come out here one more time, one of you is going to jail."***Psychological Defense Mechanism:**When the primary living environment becomes inherently hostile, the analytical brain discards verbal communication—which it views as volatile and uncalibrated—and replaces it with physical compartmentalization, spatial retreat, and absolute silence.

##### **~~1.3 The 22-Day Blackout: Parental Alienation and Systemic Breakdown~~**

The eventual breakdown of my parents' marriage manifested as an intense legal and emotional custody battle. The fragility of the child's position in domestic litigation was exposed when I picked up an extension telephone line in the house and overheard my mother’s attorney discussing tactical maneuvers:  *"If you win over Nate, you will win full custody."*  The legal strategy explicitly relied on securing the primary child as leverage. She was also advised:  *"If you leave without the kids, it shows you must trust them with their father."*This dynamic culminated in a sudden abduction event. My mother informed my siblings (Jon, Rebecca) and me that we were departing for an hour-long church function. Instead, she moved us into the residence of Gunner Johnson—a long-term friend of my father who had developed romantic interests in my mother.What followed was a 22-day communications blackout. For over three weeks, we were strictly forbidden from making telephone contact with our father. This period of enforced alienation ended only when a family court judge issued a strict order mandating our return to our father's care. To mask the systemic failure, my mother frame-shifted her actions as protective. However, the legal rupture permanently altered our living arrangements: homeschooling was terminated immediately, and I was abruptly inserted into the public school system for the 9th grade.The volatile turbulence of my early home life thus came to a close, forcing an immediate, unbuffered transition into the structured challenge of public high school.

#### 4\. The Physical Catalyst & The Chemical Escape

##### **~~4.1 Medical Trauma at Highland Hospital: The Pneumothorax Emergency~~**

In late 2008, while an undergraduate mechanical engineering student at RIT (roughly age 21–22), my reliance on logical control was completely shattered by a physical failure. Returning home from university classes, my mother looked at my pale, distressed face and asked,  *"Did you get another speeding ticket again?"*  I replied,  *"No, but I think I need to go to the hospital."*  I was suffering a spontaneous pneumothorax—a full collapse of the lung caused by air leaking into the pleural space between the lung and chest wall.Upon my arrival at Highland Hospital in Rochester, ER medical personnel initially dismissed my self-reported severe pain, asserting that if my lung were truly collapsed, I would present with far more overt physical distress. Relying on my own diagnostic intuition, I insisted on an immediate X-ray. Two hours later, two physicians entered the room carrying consent documentation and surgical equipment.The X-ray revealed a critical tension pneumothorax: trapped air in the chest cavity was building continuous pressure against the chest wall, threatening to shift internal structures and compress the heart. The required intervention involved manually cutting through my chest wall to insert a 1/4-inch vacuum chest tube designed to evacuate the trapped air and re-expand the organ. I underwent the agonizing procedure and spent three days hospitalized with a vacuum system actively pulling blood and fluid from my side.

##### **~~4.2 Biological Deconstruction: The PVC Impalement and Total Helplessness~~**

On the third day of hospitalization, medical staff performed an X-ray, removed the 1/4-inch chest tube, and pronounced the issue resolved. The instant the tube was withdrawn, I physically felt my lung re-collapse inside my chest. I immediately informed the attending physician that the vacuum had been removed prematurely before the visceral pleura had sealed. The physician dismissed my analysis, pulling professional rank:  *"I'm the doctor."*A subsequent scan confirmed my diagnosis. The physician then returned to execute a far more invasive procedure. The secondary intervention was crude and barbaric: the doctor inserted a  **1-inch diameter PVC tube, cut at a sharp 45-degree angle** , impaling it directly between my ribs on my right side.For an engineering student who spent his days calculating fluid dynamics, structural statics, and material shear stresses, the visceral horror of having my own living flesh forcibly distorted by a crude plastic pipe was overwhelming. The mechanics of statics and strength of materials were being violently applied to my own chest. This secondary procedure required seven additional days of absolute bed rest, bringing my total stay to ten days.During one night, waking up in intolerable agony from the plastic pipe wedged between my ribs, I reached out to press the call button for the attending nurse. The pain spiked so violently that I fainted dead away before my finger could depress the switch. For a mind built on total self-reliance, this complete loss of bodily autonomy stripped away every logical control mechanism I possessed.

The drug did not merely block pain; it provided complete release from all physical and mental agony.Upon discharge, I was transitioned to outpatient pain management and prescribed 10mg Oxycodone pills (known colloquially on the street as "pinks," whereas 20mg pills were "grays" and 30mg pills were "blues"). ##### **~~5.3 Lip Service and "Stinking Thinking": Dostoevsky and Self-Deception~~**

Despite understanding this cognitive framework, addiction exposed my capacity for self-deception. I engaged heavily in what recovery literature terms "stinking thinking" and "stacking"—the habit of comparing my own drug use to more severe addicts (such as street heroin users) to artificially minimize my own condition.In my journals, I referenced Russian novelist Fyodor Dostoevsky, who famously observed that  *"swallowing so easily coexists with sincere feeling."*  This paradox describes the phenomenon of  **"lip service"** —offering insincere, wishful statements to others and oneself to preserve an illusion of control. Unlike pure lying (which is conscious, deliberate fraud), lip service is a form of half-believed self-delusion where the addict hopes their promises will magically come true without taking structural action. My left-brain executive functioning was hijacked by the Beast, constructing elegant rationalizations to justify chemical use while maintaining a facade of moral purity.This tendency to externalize blame extended to my educational finances. When my mother remarried Gerard Eberhardt—a millionaire—federal financial aid calculations changed drastically. Because step-parent income was factored into FAFSA calculations until age 27, my out-of-pocket tuition costs at RIT skyrocketed overnight from  **\$500 every 10 weeks to \$5,500 every 10 weeks (an 11-fold increase)** . Meanwhile, I watched a classmate with  \$C+\$  grades, whose family was going through bankruptcy, receive  **\+\$2,500 every 10 weeks**  in direct financial aid payouts. This stark contrast reinforced my perception of a deeply unfair, ill-logical system, even as I maintained straight-A academic performance.This deep examination of personal and financial systems naturally expanded into an analysis of broader societal structures.
